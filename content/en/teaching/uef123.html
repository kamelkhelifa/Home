Syllabus

Course description

This course explores the fundamental principles of oscillatory systems and wave phenomena, essential for understanding a wide range of physical systems. Topics include simple harmonic motion, damped and forced oscillations, and wave propagation.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Understand and describe oscillatory motion: Explain the properties and mathematical formulation of simple harmonic motion (SHM), damped and forced oscillations.
  2. Analyze wave phenomena: Apply the principles of wave propagation, including speed, frequency, wavelength, and amplitude, to describe mechanical and electromagnetic waves.
  3. Solve problems involving energy in oscillatory systems: Calculate the energy associated with oscillating systems and understand energy conservation in the context of oscillations.
  4. Explore resonance and damping: Analyze systems subject to resonance and damping, including the ability to predict resonance conditions and the impact of damping forces.
  5. Understand and apply wave superposition: Explain and predict the outcomes of wave interference, superposition, and standing waves, including practical applications like sound and electromagnetic waves.
  6. Use mathematical tools for wave analysis: Develop proficiency in using differential equations and trigonometric functions to model oscillatory motion and wave phenomena.
  7. Apply wave concepts to real-world situations: Interpret and model physical systems such as pendulums, springs, sound waves, and light waves in terms of vibrations and wave mechanics.
  8. Explore applications in optics and acoustics: Relate wave phenomena to applications in optics (e.g., diffraction, interference) and acoustics (e.g., sound waves, beats).
