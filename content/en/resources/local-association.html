Before founding

Sincerity to Allah (that this work be done purely for the sake of Allah, with no share in it for anything or anyone besides Him).
Knowing the Islamic ruling on founding an association; here is a link to a collection of the statements of the scholars on the matter, the conclusion of which is that it is permissible within certain guidelines.
Consulting the scholars and our brothers among the shaykhs on the matter, especially those with experience of associations and of how they are founded and run. For the fortunate one is he who takes heed from the example of others.
Performing istikhara, seeking Allah's guidance, about founding it.
Studying the history of associations to draw lessons and benefit from the experience of those who came before, such as the Association of Algerian Muslim Ulama.
That the aim in founding it be to benefit the Muslims and to raise the banner of the religion. The first, greatest and most lasting benefit is spreading sound Islamic knowledge, founded on the Qur'an and the Sunnah as understood by the Salaf of the Ummah: the Companions, the Successors and those who followed them. And while Islamic knowledge is at the centre of that benefit, its circle widens to include useful worldly sciences such as medicine, arithmetic, languages and others; each contributes what he can, be it much or little.

Founding
- Below are links to the main laws governing the founding of associations in Algeria. It is advisable to read them slowly and carefully, understanding the legal terms, their structure and their meaning. It may be best to study them together with someone who specialises in law (for example, a brother who has studied law).
- Anything unclear in these laws can be clarified by asking experienced people who have already founded associations. In this way the path is lit up and stumbling blocks are avoided. For example, when we set out the aims of the association in our first application to found it, the brothers drew our attention to an important point: some municipalities (the office in charge of associations) may reject the application because it contains an aim that they consider unacceptable, such as including among the association's aims the establishment of a Qur'anic school. Those who reject the application argue that this is one of the aims of religious associations, which come administratively under the Directorate/Ministry of "Religious" Affairs!! Someone might say: why not found a religious association, then? The answer is that founding one is about as difficult as it gets, unless the founders happen to be to the liking of those in charge!!!
