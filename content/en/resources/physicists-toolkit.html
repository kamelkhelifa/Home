In what follows below I shall list some of the most important tools that a (undergraduate) physicist should have or consider to have, particularly if he is aiming at doing a PhD and becoming a member of the physics research community.

It is highly advised that the undergrad physicist begins his journey to master the below-mentioned points as soon as he gets admitted into a university; the rule is "the sooner the better". This gives him the opportunity to spend the bulk of his Master and PhD time focusing on the physics rather than on linguistics and/or computer science.

I. English

English language is strongly recommended these days for any sought scientific development both at the individual and community levels. It should, however, be understood that this should never mean that individuals and/or communities are to replace their native language by the English one. History has shown us that languages go up and down, just like nations!

1. Operating System (OS) : Linux/Ubuntu

The Linux OS, being an open source (free) operating system with enormous community, is much preferred over all other OSs in the market (such as Windows and Macintosh). The Ubuntu distribution is recommended for newbies.

Installation:

  • Go to http://www.ubuntu.com/download
  • Click on "Ubuntu Desktop"
  • Choose latest version in "For extended support"
  • Follow the instructions
2. Type-setting Programs: Latex

Type-setting programs, also known as Document Preparation Software (DPS), provide a helpful platform for the production of high quality scientific work. The LATEX is the most recommended DPS program for publications in physics, mathematics, chemistry and many others.
