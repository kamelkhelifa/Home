Example 27.9 · Charging a Capacitor in an RC Circuit

Throw the switch and the capacitor charges — quickly at first, then ever more slowly — while the current dies away. Everything is set by one number, the time constant \(\tau=RC\).

Problem

An uncharged capacitor and a resistor are connected in series to a battery as shown in Figure 27.15, where \(\varepsilon=12.0\) V, \(C=5.00\ \mu\text{F}\) and \(R=8.00\times10^5\ \Omega\). The switch is thrown to position \(a\). Find the time constant of the circuit, the maximum charge on the capacitor, the maximum current in the circuit, and the charge and current as functions of time.

Conceptualize & Categorize

Throwing the switch to position \(a\) (Figure 27.15b) starts the charging. The results follow from equations developed in this section — a substitution problem.

Analyze

  1. Time constant (Equation 27.20): \[\tau=RC=(8.00\times10^5\ \Omega)(5.00\times10^{-6}\ \text{F})=4.00\ \text{s}.\]
  2. Maximum charge, as \(t\to\infty\) (Equation 27.17): \[Q=C\varepsilon=(5.00\ \mu\text{F})(12.0\ \text{V})=60.0\ \mu\text{C}.\]
  3. Maximum current, at \(t=0\) (Equation 27.16): \[I_i=\frac{\varepsilon}{R}=\frac{12.0\ \text{V}}{8.00\times10^5\ \Omega}=15.0\ \mu\text{A}.\]
  4. Equations 27.18 and 27.19, with \(q\) in microcoulombs, \(i\) in microamperes and \(t\) in seconds: \[q(t)=60.0\left(1-e^{-t/4.00}\right),\qquad i(t)=15.0\,e^{-t/4.00}.\]
Going furtherWhere does the battery’s energy go?

While charging to \(Q=C\varepsilon\), the battery does work \(Q\varepsilon=C\varepsilon^2\). The capacitor ends up storing \(\tfrac12C\varepsilon^2\) — exactly half. The other half, \(\int_0^\infty i^2R\,dt=\tfrac12C\varepsilon^2\), is dissipated in the resistor, whatever the value of \(R\). A smaller \(R\) charges faster with a larger current, and the two effects cancel.

CheckCheck the numbers

The charging equation \(\varepsilon-q/C-R\,dq/dt=0\) (the loop rule) stepped forward numerically with 4th-order Runge–Kutta, against the exact solution.
