Example 27.4 · Find the Equivalent Resistance

Two resistors in series, then a parallel pair: the network collapses in two steps to a single resistor. Change any of the five numbers and watch both the reduction and the currents follow.

Problem

Four resistors are connected as shown in Figure 27.8a. (A) Find the equivalent resistance between points \(a\) and \(c\). (B) What is the current in each resistor if a potential difference of 42 V is maintained between \(a\) and \(c\)?

InteractiveReduce, then rebuild
  1. Make the 3.0-Ω resistor very large. The parallel pair tends to the 6.0-Ω resistor alone, and almost all the current goes through it.
  2. Make it very small: it shorts out the 6.0-Ω resistor. Which current tends to zero?
  3. Whatever you choose, \(I_1/I_2\) equals the inverse ratio of the two parallel resistances.
SolutionStep-by-step solution, as on the slides

Conceptualize

All charges pass from \(a\) to \(b\) through the first two resistors, but split at \(b\) into two paths through the 6.0-Ω and 3.0-Ω resistors.

Categorize

The combination is simple enough to use the rules for series and parallel combinations of resistors.

Analyze — part (A)

  1. The 8.0-Ω and 4.0-Ω resistors are in series: \[R_{\text{eq}}=8.0\ \Omega+4.0\ \Omega=12.0\ \Omega.\]
  2. The 6.0-Ω and 3.0-Ω resistors are in parallel: \[\frac1{R_{\text{eq}}}=\frac1{6.0\ \Omega}+\frac1{3.0\ \Omega}=\frac{3}{6.0\ \Omega}\;\Rightarrow\;R_{\text{eq}}=\frac{6.0\ \Omega}{3}=2.0\ \Omega.\]
  3. The circuit now looks like Figure 27.8b; the 12.0-Ω and 2.0-Ω resistors are in series: \[R_{\text{eq}}=12.0\ \Omega+2.0\ \Omega=14.0\ \Omega.\]

Analyze — part (B)

  1. The currents in the 8.0-Ω and 4.0-Ω resistors are the same because they are in series. Use Equation 26.7 and the result of part (A): \[I=\frac{\Delta V_{ac}}{R_{\text{eq}}}=\frac{42\ \text{V}}{14.0\ \Omega}=3.0\ \text{A}.\]
  2. Set the voltages across the resistors in parallel equal: \[\Delta V_1=\Delta V_2\;\Rightarrow\;(6.0\ \Omega)I_1=(3.0\ \Omega)I_2\;\Rightarrow\;I_2=2.0\,I_1.\]
  3. Use \(I_1+I_2=3.0\) A to find \(I_1\), then \(I_2\): \[I_1+2.0I_1=3.0\ \text{A}\;\Rightarrow\;I_1=1.0\ \text{A},\qquad I_2=2.0(1.0\ \text{A})=2.0\ \text{A}.\]

Finalize

As a final check, the potential differences across the two pieces must add up to the 42 V applied between \(a\) and \(c\): \(\Delta V_{ab}=(3.0\ \text{A})(12.0\ \Omega)=36\ \text{V}\) and \(\Delta V_{bc}=(3.0\ \text{A})(2.0\ \Omega)=6.0\ \text{V}\), so \(\Delta V_{ac}=36+6.0=42\ \text{V}\). The check closes.

CheckCheck the numbers

Independent of the reduction: solve the circuit directly with Kirchhoff’s rules (node \(b\) is the only unknown potential), then compare.
