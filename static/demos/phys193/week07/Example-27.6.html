Example 27.6 · A Single-Loop Circuit

Guess a direction for the current, walk once around the loop adding the rises and drops, and solve. If the guess was wrong, the answer simply comes out negative. Flip either battery and see the sign rules at work.

Problem

A single-loop circuit contains two resistors and two batteries as shown in Figure 27.13. (Neglect the internal resistances of the batteries.) Find the current in the circuit.

InteractiveWalk around the loop
SolutionStep-by-step solution, as on the slides

Conceptualize

The 12-V battery is the stronger of the two, so the current should be counterclockwise. We nevertheless guess clockwise (Figure 27.13), expecting the guess to be wrong, and see how the final answer represents that.

Categorize

Kirchhoff’s rules are not needed for so simple a circuit, but we use them to see how they apply. There are no junctions, so the current is the same in every element.

Analyze

  1. Assume the current is clockwise. Traversing clockwise from \(a\): \(a\to b\) is \(+\varepsilon_1\), \(b\to c\) is \(-IR_1\), \(c\to d\) is \(-\varepsilon_2\), \(d\to a\) is \(-IR_2\). Apply the loop rule: \[\sum\Delta V=0\;\Rightarrow\;\varepsilon_1-IR_1-\varepsilon_2-IR_2=0.\]
  2. Solve for \(I\) and use the values in Figure 27.13: \[I=\frac{\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2}{R_1+R_2}=\frac{6.0\ \text{V}-12\ \text{V}}{8.0\ \Omega+10\ \Omega}=-0.33\ \text{A}.\qquad(1)\]

Finalize

The negative sign means the current is opposite to the assumed direction. The emfs in the numerator subtract because the batteries have opposite polarities; the resistances in the denominator add because the resistors are in series.

What if?What if the polarity of the 12.0-V battery were reversed?

Rather than repeat the loop rule, modify Equation (1): the two batteries now point the same way, so the signs of \(\varepsilon_1\) and \(\varepsilon_2\) are the same: \[I=\frac{\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2}{R_1+R_2}=\frac{6.0\ \text{V}+12\ \text{V}}{8.0\ \Omega+10\ \Omega}=1.0\ \text{A}.\] Use the button to reverse it in the circuit above.
