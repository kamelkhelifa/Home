Example 30.7 · emf Induced in a Generator

Spin a coil in a field and the flux through it goes up and down like a cosine, so the emf comes out as a sine. Its peak is \(NAB\omega\): more turns, more area, more field or faster spinning, and the peak grows in proportion.

Problem

The coil in an AC generator consists of 8 turns of wire, each of area \(A=0.090\,0\ \text{m}^2\), and the total resistance of the wire is 12.0 Ω. The coil rotates in a 0.500-T magnetic field at a constant frequency of 60.0 Hz. (A) Find the maximum induced emf in the coil. (B) What is the maximum induced current in the coil when the output terminals are connected to a low-resistance conductor?

Conceptualize

Study Figure 30.18 to make sure you understand the operation of an AC generator.

Categorize

We evaluate parameters using equations developed in this section, so we categorize this example as a substitution problem.

Analyze

  1. (A) Use Equation 30.11 to find the maximum induced emf: \[\varepsilon_{\max}=NAB\omega=NAB(2\pi f).\]
  2. Substitute numerical values: \[\varepsilon_{\max}=8(0.090\,0\ \text{m}^2)(0.500\ \text{T})(2\pi)(60.0\ \text{Hz})=136\ \text{V}.\]
  3. (B) Use Equation 26.7 and the result of part (A): \[I_{\max}=\frac{\varepsilon_{\max}}{R}=\frac{136\ \text{V}}{12.0\ \Omega}=11.3\ \text{A}.\]
Going furtherWhy is the emf zero when the coil faces the field squarely, and largest when it is edge-on?

Faraday’s law cares about the rate of change of the flux, not its size. When the coil faces the field (\(\theta=0\)) the flux is at its maximum and, for an instant, not changing, so the emf is zero. A quarter turn later the flux is zero but is changing fastest, so the emf is at its peak. Flux and emf are a quarter cycle apart. You can see the same thing in the motional-emf picture: at \(\theta=90^\circ\) the two long sides of the coil cut straight across the field lines at full speed \(v=\omega(\ell/2)\), and each gives \(B\ell v\).

CheckCheck the numbers

The emf obtained by differentiating the flux \(NAB\cos\omega t\) numerically (central differences) against \(NAB\omega\sin\omega t\), and the peak found by scanning one cycle.
