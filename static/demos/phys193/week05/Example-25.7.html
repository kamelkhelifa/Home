Example 25.7 · Effect of a Metallic Slab

A metal slab in the gap carries no field inside, so the field only has the two air gaps to cross: it is as if the plates were closer by the slab's thickness. Move the slab up and down and the capacitance does not change at all.

Problem

A parallel-plate capacitor has a plate separation \(d\) and plate area \(A\). An uncharged metallic slab of thickness \(a\) is inserted midway between the plates. (A) Find the capacitance of the device. (B) Show that the capacitance of the original capacitor is unaffected by the insertion of the metallic slab if the slab is infinitesimally thin.

InteractiveTwo capacitors in series
SolutionStep-by-step solution, as on the slides

Conceptualize

Figure 25.20a shows the metallic slab between the plates of the capacitor. Any charge that appears on one plate of the capacitor must induce a charge of equal magnitude and opposite sign on the near side of the slab as shown in Figure 25.20a. Consequently, the net charge on the slab remains zero and the electric field inside the slab is zero.

Categorize

The planes of charge on the metallic slab's upper and lower edges are identical to the distribution of charges on the plates of a capacitor. The metal between the slab's edges serves only to make an electrical connection between the edges. Therefore, we can model the edges of the slab as conducting planes and the bulk of the slab as a wire. As a result, the capacitor in Figure 25.20a is equivalent to two capacitors in series, each having a plate separation \((d-a)/2\) as shown in Figure 25.20b.

Analyze

  1. (A) Use Equation 25.3 and the rule for adding two capacitors in series (Eq. 25.12) to find the equivalent capacitance in Figure 25.20b: \[\frac{1}{C}=\frac{1}{C_1}+\frac{1}{C_2}=\frac{1}{\dfrac{\epsilon_0A}{(d-a)/2}}+\frac{1}{\dfrac{\epsilon_0A}{(d-a)/2}}.\]
  2. Simplify: \[C=\frac{\epsilon_0A}{d-a}.\]
  3. (B) In the result for part (A), let \(a\to0\): \[C=\lim_{a\to0}\frac{\epsilon_0A}{d-a}=\frac{\epsilon_0A}{d}.\]

Finalize

The result of part (B) is the original capacitance before the slab is inserted, which tells us that we can insert an infinitesimally thin metallic sheet between the plates of a capacitor without affecting the capacitance. We use this fact in the next example.

What if?What if the metallic slab in part (A) is not midway between the plates? How would that affect the capacitance?

Let the distance between the slab's upper edge and the upper plate be \(b\); the distance between its lower edge and the lower plate is then \(d-b-a\). As in part (A), \[\frac{1}{C}=\frac{b}{\epsilon_0A}+\frac{d-b-a}{\epsilon_0A}=\frac{d-a}{\epsilon_0A}\ \Rightarrow\ C=\frac{\epsilon_0A}{d-a},\] the same result. The capacitance is independent of \(b\): the decrease in plate separation of one capacitor is compensated by the increase for the other. Drag the slab position slider and watch the readout.

CheckCheck the numbers

With a charge \(Q\) on the plates, the field is \(\sigma/\epsilon_0\) in the air and zero in the metal. Integrating it across the gap numerically (Simpson, with the slab's edges on panel boundaries) gives \(\Delta V\) and so \(C=Q/\Delta V\), for the slab at the top, in the middle and at the bottom; all must equal \(\epsilon_0A/(d-a)\).
