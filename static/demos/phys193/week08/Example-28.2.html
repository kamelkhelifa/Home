Example 28.2 · A Proton Moving Perpendicular to a Uniform Magnetic Field

A force that is always perpendicular to the velocity can only bend the path, and a constant bend is a circle. Setting \(qvB\) equal to \(mv^2/r\) ties the radius to the speed, and the period to nothing but \(q\), \(B\) and \(m\). Watch the proton go round, then swap it for an electron.

Problem

A proton is moving in a circular orbit of radius 14 cm in a uniform 0.35-T magnetic field perpendicular to the velocity of the proton. Find the speed of the proton.

InteractiveGo round the circle
SolutionStep-by-step solution, as on the slides

Conceptualize

From our discussion in this section, we know the proton follows a circular path when moving perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field. In Chapter 38, we will learn that the highest possible speed for a particle is the speed of light, \(3.00\times10^8\) m/s, so the speed of the particle in this problem must come out to be smaller than that value.

Categorize

The proton is described by both the particle in a field model and the particle in uniform circular motion model. These models led to Equation 28.3.

Analyze

  1. Solve Equation 28.3 for the speed of the particle: \[r=\frac{mv}{qB}\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=\frac{qBr}{m_p}.\]
  2. Substitute numerical values: \[v=\frac{(1.60\times10^{-19}\ \text{C})(0.35\ \text{T})(0.14\ \text{m})}{1.67\times10^{-27}\ \text{kg}}=4.7\times10^6\ \text{m/s}.\]

Finalize

The speed found is indeed smaller than the speed of light, as required.

What if?What if an electron, rather than a proton, moves in a direction perpendicular to the same magnetic field with this same speed? Will the radius of its orbit be different?

An electron has a much smaller mass than a proton, so the magnetic force should be able to change its velocity much more easily than that for the proton. Therefore, we expect the radius to be smaller. Equation 28.3 shows that \(r\) is proportional to \(m\) with \(q\), \(B\) and \(v\) the same for the electron as for the proton. Consequently, the radius will be smaller by the same factor as the ratio of masses \(m_e/m_p\): 14 cm becomes 0.076 mm. Press the button; the drawing rescales to the new orbit.

CheckCheck the numbers

The equation of motion \(m\,d\vec v/dt=q\vec v\times\vec B\) integrated numerically (RK4) for one period: the particle must come back to its starting point, the speed must stay constant, and the radius of the path must equal \(mv/qB\). Also the period \(2\pi m/qB\), independent of the speed.
