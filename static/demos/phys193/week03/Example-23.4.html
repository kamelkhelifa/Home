Example 23.4 · Flux Through a Cube

Flux is counted with a sign: field leaving a closed surface counts positive, field entering counts negative. Turn the cube any way you like in the uniform field and watch the six face fluxes change — and their sum stay at exactly zero.

Problem

Consider a uniform electric field \(\vec E\) oriented in the \(x\) direction in empty space. A cube of edge length \(\ell\) is placed in the field, oriented as shown in Figure 23.9. Find the net electric flux through the surface of the cube.

InteractiveSix faces, one sum
  1. Start in the textbook orientation (both angles 0). Only faces ① and ② carry flux: \(-E\ell^2\) and \(+E\ell^2\).
  2. Turn the cube. Now up to three faces let field in and three let it out — but every line that enters must leave.
  3. Double \(\ell\). Each face flux grows by 4; the net flux is still zero.
SolutionStep-by-step solution, as on the slides

Conceptualize

The field lines pass through two faces perpendicularly and are parallel to the four other faces of the cube.

Categorize

We evaluate the flux from its definition — a substitution problem. The flux through four of the faces (③, ④ and the unnumbered faces) is zero because \(\vec E\) is parallel to those faces and therefore perpendicular to \(d\vec A\) on them.

Analyze

  1. Net flux through faces ① and ②: \[\Phi_E=\int_1\vec E\cdot d\vec A+\int_2\vec E\cdot d\vec A.\]
  2. Face ①: \(\vec E\) is constant and inward, \(d\vec A_1\) is outward (\(\theta=180^\circ\)): \[\int_1\vec E\cdot d\vec A=\int_1E\,dA\cos180^\circ=-E\int_1dA=-EA=-E\ell^2.\]
  3. Face ②: \(\vec E\) is outward and along \(d\vec A_2\) (\(\theta=0^\circ\)): \[\int_2\vec E\cdot d\vec A=\int_2E\,dA\cos0^\circ=E\int_2dA=+EA=E\ell^2.\]
  4. Add all six faces: \[\Phi_E=-E\ell^2+E\ell^2+0+0+0+0=0.\]

Finalize

The net electric flux through the closed surface of the cube is zero. Section 23.4 explains why: there is no charge inside the cube.

Going furtherWhat makes the net flux non-zero?

Only charge inside the surface. Switch on a point charge below. Its contribution to each face is computed exactly from the solid angle that face subtends at the charge, \(\Phi_{\text{face}}=\dfrac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\,\Omega_{\text{face}}\). Put the charge inside and the six contributions add to \(q/\epsilon_0\) wherever it sits; take it outside and they add to zero. That is Gauss’s law (§23.4), arrived at by brute force.
