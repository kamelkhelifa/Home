Example 31.6 · Oscillations in an LC Circuit

Charge the capacitor, then connect it across the inductor and nothing dissipates: the energy sloshes back and forth between the electric field of \(C\) and the magnetic field of \(L\), forever, at \(\omega=1/\sqrt{LC}\). It is the electrical twin of a mass on a spring.

Problem

In Figure 31.11, the battery has an emf of 12.0 V, the inductance is 2.81 mH, and the capacitance is 9.00 pF. The switch has been set to position \(a\) for a long time so that the capacitor is charged. The switch is then thrown to position \(b\), removing the battery from the circuit and connecting the capacitor directly across the inductor. (A) Find the frequency of oscillation of the circuit. (B) What are the maximum values of charge on the capacitor and current in the circuit?

SolutionStep-by-step solution, as on the slides

Conceptualize

When the switch is thrown to position \(b\), the active part of the circuit is the right-hand loop, which is an LC circuit.

Categorize

We use equations developed in this section, so we categorize this example as a substitution problem.

Analyze

  1. (A) Use Equation 31.22 to find the frequency: \[f=\frac{\omega}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}.\]
  2. Substitute numerical values: \[f=\frac{1}{2\pi\left[(2.81\times10^{-3}\ \text{H})(9.00\times10^{-12}\ \text{F})\right]^{1/2}}=1.00\times10^6\ \text{Hz}.\]
  3. (B) Find the initial charge on the capacitor, which equals the maximum charge: \[Q_{\max}=C\varepsilon=(9.00\times10^{-12}\ \text{F})(12.0\ \text{V})=1.08\times10^{-10}\ \text{C}.\]
  4. Use Equation 31.23 to express the maximum current in terms of the maximum charge: \[I_{\max}=\omega Q_{\max}=2\pi fQ_{\max}=(2\pi\times10^6\ \text{s}^{-1})(1.08\times10^{-10}\ \text{C})=6.79\times10^{-4}\ \text{A}.\]
Going furtherWhere is the energy at each moment?

Start with all of it in the capacitor, \(U_E=Q_{\max}^2/2C=\tfrac12C\varepsilon^2=648\) pJ. A quarter period later the capacitor is empty and the current is at its peak; now all of it is in the inductor, \(\tfrac12LI_{\max}^2\), the same 648 pJ. The two forms are exactly the analogues of the spring’s \(\tfrac12kx^2\) and the mass’s \(\tfrac12mv^2\): \(q\leftrightarrow x\), \(i\leftrightarrow v\), \(L\leftrightarrow m\), \(1/C\leftrightarrow k\). Use the table below to see that \(U_E+U_B\) is the same at every instant to the last digit.

CheckCheck the numbers

The loop equation \(L\,di/dt+q/C=0\) with \(i=dq/dt\) integrated numerically (4th-order Runge–Kutta over one period) against \(Q_{\max}\cos\omega t\), the period measured from the numerical zero-crossings, and the total energy at several instants.
