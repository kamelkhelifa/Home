Example 32.4 · Analyzing a Series RLC Circuit

Three elements in series, one current through all of them, and three voltages that peak at three different instants. Add them as arrows, not as numbers: the phasor diagram turns the whole circuit into one right triangle, whose hypotenuse is the impedance and whose angle is the phase lag.

Problem

A series RLC circuit has \(R=425\ \Omega\), \(L=1.25\) H, and \(C=3.50\ \mu\)F. It is connected to an AC source with \(f=60.0\) Hz and \(\Delta V_{\max}=150\) V. (A) Determine the inductive reactance, the capacitive reactance, and the impedance of the circuit. (B) Find the maximum current in the circuit. (C) Find the phase angle between the current and voltage. (D) Find the maximum voltage across each element. (E) What replacement value of \(L\) should an engineer analyzing the circuit choose such that the current leads the applied voltage by 30.0° rather than 34.0°? All other values in the circuit stay the same.

SolutionStep-by-step solution, as on the slides

Conceptualize

The circuit of interest in this example is shown in Figure 32.13a. The current in the combination of the resistor, inductor, and capacitor oscillates at a particular phase angle with respect to the applied voltage.

Categorize

The circuit is a simple series RLC circuit, so we can use the approach discussed in this section.

Analyze

  1. (A) Find the angular frequency: \(\omega=2\pi f=2\pi(60.0\ \text{Hz})=377\ \text{s}^{-1}\).
  2. Use Equation 32.13 to find the inductive reactance: \(X_L=\omega L=(377\ \text{s}^{-1})(1.25\ \text{H})=471\ \Omega\).
  3. Use Equation 32.21 to find the capacitive reactance: \[X_C=\frac1{\omega C}=\frac1{(377\ \text{s}^{-1})(3.50\times10^{-6}\ \text{F})}=758\ \Omega.\]
  4. Use Equation 32.28 to find the impedance: \[Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}=\sqrt{(425\ \Omega)^2+(471\ \Omega-758\ \Omega)^2}=513\ \Omega.\]
  5. (B) Use Equation 32.29 to find the maximum current: \[I_{\max}=\frac{\Delta V_{\max}}{Z}=\frac{150\ \text{V}}{513\ \Omega}=0.292\ \text{A}.\]
  6. (C) Use Equation 32.30 to find the phase angle: \[\phi=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{X_L-X_C}{R}\right)=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{471\ \Omega-758\ \Omega}{425\ \Omega}\right)=-34.0^\circ.\]
  7. (D) Use Equations 32.4, 32.14, and 32.22 to find the maximum voltage across each element: \[\Delta V_R=I_{\max}R=(0.292\ \text{A})(425\ \Omega)=124\ \text{V},\qquad \Delta V_L=I_{\max}X_L=(0.292\ \text{A})(471\ \Omega)=138\ \text{V},\] \[\Delta V_C=I_{\max}X_C=(0.292\ \text{A})(758\ \Omega)=221\ \text{V}.\]
  8. (E) Solve Equation 32.30 for the inductive reactance: \(X_L=X_C+R\tan\phi\). Substitute Equations 32.13 and 32.21 into this expression: \[\omega L=\frac1{\omega C}+R\tan\phi\quad\Rightarrow\quad L=\frac1\omega\left(\frac1{\omega C}+R\tan\phi\right).\] Substitute numerical values: \[L=\frac1{377\ \text{s}^{-1}}\left[\frac1{(377\ \text{s}^{-1})(3.50\times10^{-6}\ \text{F})}+(425\ \Omega)\tan(-30.0^\circ)\right]=1.36\ \text{H}.\]

Finalize

Because the capacitive reactance is larger than the inductive reactance, the phase angle is negative: the current leads the applied voltage. In part (E) a smaller inductance brings \(X_L\) closer to \(X_C\), so the circuit is less capacitive and the lead shrinks to 30.0°. Press the button under What if? to see the triangle change.

What if?What if you added up the maximum voltages across the three circuit elements? Is that a physical quantity?

The sum of the maximum voltages across the elements is \(\Delta V_R+\Delta V_L+\Delta V_C=484\) V. This sum is much greater than the maximum voltage of the source, 150 V. The sum of the maximum voltages is a meaningless quantity because when sinusoidally varying quantities are added, both their amplitudes and their phases must be taken into account. The maximum voltages across the various elements occur at different times, so the voltages must be added in a way that takes account of the different phases: that is what the phasor diagram does. \(\Delta V_L\) and \(\Delta V_C\) point in opposite directions and largely cancel, and the remaining \(\Delta V_L-\Delta V_C=-83\) V combines with \(\Delta V_R=124\) V at right angles to give exactly 150 V. Tick the box above to see the naive sum laid end to end next to the true phasor sum.

CheckCheck the numbers

The loop rule integrated directly: \(L\,di/dt+Ri+q/C=\Delta V_{\max}\sin\omega t\), stepped numerically (RK4) through many cycles until the transient has died away, then the steady-state amplitude and phase of the current are measured and compared with \(\Delta V_{\max}/Z\) and \(\tan^{-1}[(X_L-X_C)/R]\). Also the phasor sum \(\sqrt{\Delta V_R^2+(\Delta V_L-\Delta V_C)^2}\), which must give back \(\Delta V_{\max}\).
