Example 32.1 · What Is the rms Current?

An AC current averages to zero, yet it heats the resistor. The number that measures the heating is the rms current: square, average, take the root. For a sine wave that is simply \(I_{\max}/\sqrt2\). Watch the squared current fill in and its average settle at exactly one half.

Problem

The voltage output of an AC source is given by the expression \(\Delta v=200\sin\omega t\), where \(\Delta v\) is in volts. Find the rms current in the circuit when this source is connected to a 47.0-Ω resistor.

SolutionStep-by-step solution, as on the slides

Conceptualize

Figure 32.2 shows the physical situation for this problem: a single resistor across an AC source, with nothing else in the loop.

Categorize

We evaluate the current with an equation developed in this section, so we categorize this example as a substitution problem.

Analyze

  1. Combine Equations 32.4 and 32.6 to find the rms current: \[I_{\text{rms}}=\frac{I_{\max}}{\sqrt2}=\frac{\Delta V_{\max}}{\sqrt2R}.\]
  2. Comparing the expression for voltage output with the general form \(\Delta v=\Delta V_{\max}\sin\omega t\) shows that \(\Delta V_{\max}=200\) V. Substitute numerical values: \[I_{\text{rms}}=\frac{200\ \text{V}}{\sqrt2(47.0\ \Omega)}=3.01\ \text{A}.\]
Going furtherWhy is the factor \(1/\sqrt2\), and is it always?

The average of \(\sin^2\omega t\) over a cycle is exactly ½: \(\sin^2\) and \(\cos^2\) have the same average and add up to 1. So \((i^2)_{\text{avg}}=\tfrac12I_{\max}^2\) and \(I_{\text{rms}}=I_{\max}/\sqrt2=0.707I_{\max}\). The factor belongs to the sine wave, not to AC in general: a square wave switching between \(\pm I_{\max}\) has \(I_{\text{rms}}=I_{\max}\), and a triangle wave has \(I_{\max}/\sqrt3\). Pick those waveforms above and watch the average level move. In every case the resistor heats as if a steady current \(I_{\text{rms}}\) flowed: \(P_{\text{avg}}=I_{\text{rms}}^2R\).

CheckCheck the numbers

The average of \(i^2\) over one cycle computed numerically (Simpson’s rule) and compared with \(\tfrac12I_{\max}^2\); the rms current from it against \(\Delta V_{\max}/\sqrt2R\); and the average power two ways, \(\langle i^2R\rangle\) and \(I_{\text{rms}}^2R\).
