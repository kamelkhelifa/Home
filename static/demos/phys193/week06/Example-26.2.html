Example 26.2 · The Resistance of Nichrome Wire

Resistance belongs to an object and resistivity to the material it is made of; \(R=\rho\ell/A\) connects them. Swap Nichrome for copper and the same wire passes sixty times the current. Change the material, the thickness and the length, and see how each one changes \(R\) and \(I\).

Problem

The radius of 22-gauge Nichrome wire is 0.32 mm. (A) Calculate the resistance per unit length of this wire. (B) If a potential difference of 10 V is maintained across a 1.0-m length of the Nichrome wire, what is the current in the wire?

SolutionStep-by-step solution, as on the slides

Conceptualize

Table 26.2 shows that Nichrome has a resistivity two orders of magnitude larger than the best conductors in the table. Therefore, we expect it to have some special practical applications that the best conductors may not have.

Categorize

We model the wire as a cylinder so that a simple geometric analysis can be applied to find the resistance.

Analyze

  1. (A) Use Equation 26.10 and the resistivity of Nichrome from Table 26.2 to find the resistance per unit length: \[\frac{R}{\ell}=\frac{\rho}{A}=\frac{\rho}{\pi r^2}=\frac{1.0\times10^{-6}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}}{\pi(0.32\times10^{-3}\ \text{m})^2}=3.1\ \Omega/\text{m}.\]
  2. (B) Use Equation 26.7 to find the current in a 1.0-m section of the wire: \[I=\frac{\Delta V}{R}=\frac{\Delta V}{(R/\ell)\,\ell}=\frac{10\ \text{V}}{(3.1\ \Omega/\text{m})(1.0\ \text{m})}=3.2\ \text{A}.\]

Finalize

Because of its high resistivity and resistance to oxidation, Nichrome is often used for heating elements in toasters, irons and electric heaters.

What if?What if the wire were copper instead of Nichrome?

Copper's resistivity is two orders of magnitude smaller, so the resistance per unit length is smaller and the current larger. A copper wire of the same radius has only \(0.053\ \Omega/\text{m}\), and a 1.0-m length carries 190 A with 10 V across it. Press What if: copper above. (A real copper wire would melt or trip a breaker long before: 190 A in a 0.64-mm wire is far beyond its rating.)

CheckCheck the numbers

The wire treated as a network: it is cut into 24 slices along its length, each slice into 6 parallel strands of equal area, neighbouring strands are joined by cross links, and the voltage at every inner junction is solved from Kirchhoff's junction rule (a linear system, Gaussian elimination). The total current that flows in for the applied \(\Delta V\) gives the resistance, compared with \(\rho\ell/A\).
