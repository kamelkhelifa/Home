Example 33.2 · An Electromagnetic Wave

In a plane wave travelling along \(x\), \(\vec E\) and \(\vec B\) oscillate in phase, at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel, and their sizes are locked together: \(E=cB\) at every point and every instant. One frequency fixes the wavelength and the period; one field amplitude fixes the other.

Problem

A sinusoidal electromagnetic wave of frequency 40.0 MHz travels in free space in the \(x\) direction as in Figure 33.9. (A) Determine the wavelength and period of the wave. (B) At some point and at some instant, the electric field has its maximum value of 750 N/C and is directed along the \(y\) axis. Calculate the magnitude and direction of the magnetic field at this position and time.

InteractiveThe travelling wave
SolutionStep-by-step solution, as on the slides

Conceptualize

Imagine the wave in Figure 33.9 moving to the right along the \(x\) axis, with the electric and magnetic fields oscillating in phase.

Categorize

We use the mathematical representation of the traveling wave model for electromagnetic waves.

Analyze (A)

  1. Solve Equation 16.12 to find the wavelength of the wave: \[\lambda=\frac cf=\frac{3.00\times10^8\ \text{m/s}}{40.0\times10^6\ \text{Hz}}=7.50\ \text{m}.\]
  2. Find the period \(T\) of the wave as the inverse of the frequency: \[T=\frac1f=\frac{1}{40.0\times10^6\ \text{Hz}}=2.50\times10^{-8}\ \text{s}.\]

Analyze (B)

  1. Use Equation 33.24 to find the magnitude of the magnetic field: \[B_\text{max}=\frac{E_\text{max}}{c}=\frac{750\ \text{N/C}}{3.00\times10^8\ \text{m/s}}=2.50\times10^{-6}\ \text{T}.\]
  2. Because \(\vec E\) and \(\vec B\) must be perpendicular to each other and perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation (\(x\) in this case), we conclude that \(\vec B\) is in the \(z\) direction.

Finalize

Notice that the wavelength is several meters. This is relatively long for an electromagnetic wave. As we will see in Section 33.7, this wave belongs to the radio range of frequencies.

Going furtherPlus or minus \(z\)?

The direction of travel is the direction of \(\vec E\times\vec B\) (the Poynting vector of §33.4). With \(\vec E\) along \(+y\) and the wave going along \(+x\), \(\hat\jmath\times\hat k=\hat\imath\) requires \(\vec B\) along \(+z\) at that point. Press Reverse the wave: with the wave going along \(-x\), \(\vec B\) at a crest of \(\vec E\) points along \(-z\) instead. Then move the frequency across the spectrum: the same relations hold from radio waves to gamma rays.

CheckCheck the numbers

The fields \(E_y=E_\text{max}\cos(kx-\omega t)\) and \(B_z=B_\text{max}\cos(kx-\omega t)\) put into Maxwell's equations for a plane wave, Equations 33.16 and 33.17 (\(\partial E/\partial x=-\partial B/\partial t\) and \(\partial B/\partial x=-\mu_0\epsilon_0\,\partial E/\partial t\)), with every derivative taken by central finite differences at sample points. Both residuals vanish only when \(\omega/k=1/\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}\) and \(E_\text{max}/B_\text{max}=c\).
